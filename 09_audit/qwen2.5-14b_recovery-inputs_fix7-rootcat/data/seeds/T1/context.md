# UAW Big Three Auto Strike 2023: Pre-Resolution Intelligence Dossier

**Question:** Will the UAW reach a contract agreement with all three Big Three automakers (GM, Ford, and Stellantis) before November 1, 2023?
**Market Opened:** September 2023
**Information Cutoff:** October 30, 2023 (48 hours before the deadline)

---

## 1.0 Executive Summary & Resolution Criteria

**Summary:** On September 15, 2023, the United Auto Workers (UAW) — led by newly elected militant president Shawn Fain — launched an unprecedented "Stand-Up Strike" against all three Detroit automakers simultaneously for the first time in the union's 88-year history. The strike began with targeted shutdowns at three assembly plants and expanded in waves, threatening the entire North American auto industry. This market assesses whether the UAW's aggressive, social-movement-style campaign will force all three companies to the table before the November 1 deadline.

**Resolution Contract:** This market resolves "YES" if the UAW publicly announces ratified, tentative contract agreements with General Motors, Ford Motor Company, and Stellantis (Jeep/Ram/Dodge parent), covering the four-year wage and benefits period, with all three deals announced on or before October 31, 2023 (11:59 PM ET). It resolves "NO" if any one of the three agreements is not formally ratified or announced by the deadline.

## 2.0 Key Social Indicators (Snapshot as of Cutoff)

*Note: This dispute is driven by public solidarity, social media campaigns, and union membership voting behavior — not purely economic data.*
- **Workers on Strike (Peak):** ~46,000 UAW members across assembly and parts facilities.
- **Public Approval of UAW Strike (Gallup, Sept 2023):** 75% of Americans support the strikers — a historic high for union approval.
- **Presidential Engagement:** President Biden and former President Trump both traveled to Michigan to address workers on the picket line (first sitting president to do so since FDR era).
- **Ford Status (Oct 26):** Tentative agreement reached. ~57,000 Ford UAW members to vote.
- **GM Status (Oct 30):** Tentative agreement reached. Strike ending.
- **Stellantis Status (Oct 28):** Tentative agreement reached. Final holdout resolved.

## 3.0 Factual Reconstruction (Timeline of Social Escalation)

- **March 2023:** Shawn Fain, a militant reformer backed by the "Unite All Workers for Democracy" (UAWD) caucus, defeats incumbent president Ray Curry. His explicit platform: a historic "Big Three at once" confrontational strategy — a radical shift from the incremental approach of prior UAW leadership.
- **July 2023:** UAW issues a landmark demand list: 40% pay increases over four years, restoration of cost-of-living adjustments (eliminated since 2009), restoration of defined-benefit pensions for new hires, and a 32-hour workweek with 40-hour pay. Companies call the demands "economically impossible."
- **September 15, 2023:** Strike begins. Fain uses social media (TikTok, Instagram Lives, Facebook) and town-hall livestreams to directly address members, bypassing traditional union communication structures. His rhetoric is explicitly anti-corporate and class-conscious, drawing massive organic online solidarity.
- **September 22–29:** Fain expands the strike in dramatic "Stand Up" announcement calls, adding new plants each Friday as a pressure tactic. The expansion of strikes to GM's Flint Engine and Ford's Chicago Assembly creates massive supply chain disruptions for high-margin trucks and SUVs.
- **October 11:** UAW expands strike to all 38 US GM parts distribution centers. The industry supply chain faces a full disruption of parts availability. The ripple effect threatens hundreds of downstream auto parts suppliers and their workers.
- **October 24–30:** The final week sees simultaneous negotiations with all three automakers, with Fain personally announcing each breakthrough via social media livestream. The public mobilization and $825 million UAW strike fund create the financial leverage necessary for Fain's strategy.

## 4.0 Structural Mechanics of Social Pressure

**The "Stand-Up Strike" Model:**
Rather than calling all workers out at once (depleting the strike fund rapidly), Fain's wave model targeted high-profit plants (trucks: F-150 Lightning, Silverado, Ram) first.
- *The Tactic:* Companies cannot easily absorb a partial strike. Not knowing which plant comes next forces all three companies to the table simultaneously, preventing any one company from being a "strike breaker" by reaching a separate side-deal.

**The Social Media Amplification Loop:**
Fain's weekly livestreams averaged millions of views. Each strike expansion announcement created a viral news cycle, generating sustained public attention and politician engagement.
- *The Effect:* Both Biden and Trump physically joining picket lines transformed the labor dispute into a broader class-and-wages cultural moment, sustaining strike morale and public pressure.

**The Presidential Election Dimension:**
Michigan is a critical swing state with ~160,000 UAW members. Both major political parties recognized that publicly opposing the strike would be electoral suicide in the Rust Belt, removing the political pressure valve that historically dampened union demands.

## 5.0 Divergence: Who Settles First?

**Ford (Likely First Mover):**
Ford faced the starkest vulnerability. Their Michigan Assembly plant (F-150 Lightning and Bronco) and Chicago Assembly (Explorer, Aviator) were on strike, threatening their two highest-margin product lines. Ford also had the most progressive labor relations history among the Big Three.

**Stellantis (Aggressive Holdout):**
CEO Carlos Tavares took the hardest public line, publicly accusing the UAW of seeking to "destroy" the industry. Stellantis had the smallest North American market share and theoretically the least to lose from a prolonged strike. However, the Jeep Wrangler and Ram Truck assembly in Toledo and Sterling Heights were vulnerable.

**GM (Strategic Middle Ground):**
CEO Mary Barra publicly engaged with negotiators but refused major pension restoration demands. GM's vulnerability was its national parts distribution network being struck — a move threatening dealerships and the secondary market, not just assembly.

## 6.0 Key Unknowns & Catalyst Triggers

1. **The EV Carve-Out Battle:** UAW's core demand included extending full union contracts to EV battery plants (currently structured as separate joint ventures at lower wages). If automakers resist extending UAW contracts to cover battery facilities (their growth investment), the EV provision could be the final deal-breaker.
2. **Member Ratification Risk:** Even if leadership reaches tentative agreements, the rank-and-file membership must ratify each deal by vote. A rejection from a major faction (as occurred in 2019 when GM members narrowly ratified) could collapse the deal and force renegotiation past the deadline.
3. **Federal Intervention:** The White House repeatedly signaled it would not invoke Taft-Hartley to force workers back, but extraordinary economic damage (e.g., suppliers laying off 10,000+ workers) could change the political calculus and trigger federal mediation.

## 7.0 Scenario Projections

**Scenario A: The Full Sweep (Resolves YES — Base Case)**
- **Thesis:** The combination of public pressure, a well-funded strike fund, targeted disruption of high-profit vehicles, and presidential political exposure forces all three companies to capitulate. Fain's social-movement model succeeds.
- **Projected Outcome:** All three tentative agreements are reached in the final two weeks of October. Workers ratify the deals. The UAW achieves approximately 25% wage increases over four years and recovers COLA provisions.

**Scenario B: The Stellantis Holdout (Resolves NO — Tail Risk)**
- **Thesis:** Tavares digs in. Stellantis, with its European parent (Peugeot S.A.), is more comfortable with prolonged labor disputes and has lower North American profit dependency. Stellantis and UAW do not reach an agreement until after November 1.
- **Projected Outcome:** Ford and GM settle but Stellantis's intransigence prevents a full resolution, resolving the market NO despite the social victory achieved at two of three companies.

**Scenario C: The Rank-and-File Rejection (Resolves NO — Tail Risk)**
- **Thesis:** Tentative agreements are reached, but the activist membership base, energized by Fain's rhetoric and emboldened by public support, votes to reject settlements as insufficient — demanding the full 40% wage increase and full pension restoration.
- **Projected Outcome:** Rejection of any one deal requires renegotiation, extending the strike past November 1 and resolving the market NO.
