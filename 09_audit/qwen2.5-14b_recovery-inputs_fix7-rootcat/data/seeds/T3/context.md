# 2024 Taiwanese Presidential Election: Pre-Resolution Intelligence Dossier

**Question:** Which party will win the most votes in the 2024 Taiwanese presidential election?
**Market Opened:** November 2023
**Information Cutoff:** January 12, 2024 (Eve of Election Day)

---

## 1.0 Executive Summary & Resolution Criteria

**Summary:** The January 13, 2024 Taiwanese presidential election was one of the most consequential democratic contests in the Indo-Pacific, a three-way race between the ruling Democratic Progressive Party (DPP), the Kuomintang (KMT), and the newly formed Taiwan People's Party (TPP). The election served as a de facto referendum on Taiwan's geopolitical identity — formal cross-Strait engagement with China (KMT), status quo assertiveness (DPP), or reformist domestic politics (TPP). This event is ideal for social simulation because the three-way split created highly dynamic coalition pressures, third-party spoiler dynamics, and intense social media mobilization across generational and regional lines.

**Resolution Contract:** This market resolves to the political party whose presidential candidate receives the highest share of the total valid votes cast in the January 13, 2024 Republic of China (Taiwan) presidential election, as officially certified by the Central Election Commission (CEC). The resolution is based on first-preference vote share, not legislative seats.

## 2.0 Key Social & Political Indicators (Snapshot as of Cutoff)

*Note: This election features intensely personal and ideologically-driven voter behavior, shaped by generational identity, cross-Strait threat perception, and domestic corruption narratives.*
- **DPP (William Lai "Lai Ching-te") polling average (Jan 2024):** ~33–35%.
- **KMT (Hou Yu-ih) polling average:** ~27–30%.
- **TPP (Ko Wen-je) polling average:** ~20–25%.
- **Social media activity:** TPP's youth-oriented digital campaign rivaling DPP in online engagement despite lower resource base.
- **PRC influence concern:** Taiwanese intelligence agencies flagged an unprecedented volume of disinformation campaigns on PTT (Taiwan's Reddit) targeting young voters.

## 3.0 Factual Reconstruction (Timeline)

- **January 2023:** William Lai (Lai Ching-te), the current Vice President and a self-described "pragmatic worker for Taiwan independence," is announced as the DPP presidential candidate. Beijing's state media immediately condemns him as a "troublemaker" and "separatist."
- **March 2023:** The KMT selects Hou Yu-ih, the popular Mayor of New Taipei City, as their candidate. His core message: economic competitiveness and reducing cross-Strait tensions to lower the risk of conflict.
- **July 2023:** Ko Wen-je, the charismatic former Mayor of Taipei and founder of the TPP (Taiwan People's Party), confirms his presidential run. Ko appeals to young voters disillusioned with both traditional parties and focuses on housing costs, bureaucratic reform, and corruption.
- **November 2023:** Failed unification talks between KMT and TPP to form a joint opposition coalition collapse spectacularly over the vice-presidential nominee selection. The split ensures a three-way race, benefiting the DPP incumbent.
- **December 2023–January 2024:** Intense ground-level campaigning. KMT holds massive rallies in southern Taiwan. DPP deploys its legendary ground-game infrastructure. TPP conducts digital-first campaigning, drawing record attendance at Ko's urban rallies among 20–40-year-old voters.

## 4.0 Structural Mechanics of the Three-Way Social Split

**The Generational Fracture:**
Taiwanese voters aged 18–34 (the "Sunflower Generation" who participated in the 2014 Sunflower Movement protests) skew heavily TPP or DPP. Voters 50+ tend to favor KMT's stability message.
- *Social Simulation Relevance:* Agents representing younger Taiwanese personas will respond very differently to PRC-related news injections than older, business-oriented personas. The generational cleavage creates high internal debate and diverse opinion formation.

**The China Threat Injection Effect:**
Any news event perceived as increasing PRC military aggression (e.g., PLAAF incursions into ADIZ, military exercises) historically shifts votes toward the DPP's stronger defense stance. Conversely, economic concerns (wages, housing costs) shift voters toward KMT or TPP.
- *Susceptibility Test:* An injection of a simulated PRC military provocation at Step 30 should produce measurable DPP preference increases in Condition B vs. Condition C.

**The Opposition Split:**
The KMT-TPP unification failure was driven by social dynamics (personality clash between Ko and KMT leadership, live-streamed negotiation drama that played out on social media). The social narrative of "ego over country" attached itself to Ko among older opposition voters, weakening his final numbers.

## 5.0 Divergence: Urban vs. Rural, North vs. South

**DPP Stronghold (Southern Taiwan):**
Tainan, Kaohsiung, and Pingtung — traditionally DPP territory with strong Taiwanese identity sentiment. Lai Ching-te serves as former Mayor of Tainan, his political home base.

**KMT Stronghold (Rural North and Mountainous Regions):**
Keelung, Miaoli, parts of Hualien — areas with stronger mainlander-descendant populations or economic ties to cross-Strait trade.

**TPP Surge (Urban Youth Centers):**
Taipei, Taichung, Taoyuan — young urban professionals concentrated in the tech and service economy who prioritize housing costs and domestic governance over geopolitical alignment.

## 6.0 Key Unknowns & Catalyst Triggers

1. **Last-Minute PRC Military Exercises:** Taiwan's military reported unusual PLAAF activities in the ADIZ in the final weeks of January 2024. Any escalation directly before Election Day could trigger a nationalistic rally around the DPP, crushing the TPP's message of domestic pragmatism.
2. **Late-Deciding Young Voters:** Polling consistently showed ~15% of voters aged 20–34 were undecided in the final week. The direction of this cohort determines whether TPP underperforms or overperforms its polling average.
3. **Turnout Differential:** High turnout historically favors DPP. Low turnout (especially among young TPP-leaning voters who are first-time participants) could hand KMT a surprise result in the popular vote.

## 7.0 Scenario Projections

**Scenario A: DPP Plurality Victory (Resolves DPP — Base Case)**
- **Thesis:** Lai Ching-te maintains his polling lead. Young undecided voters split between TPP and KMT. The PRC threat environment sustains DPP's identity-based turnout machine.
- **Projected Outcome:** Lai wins with approximately 40% of the vote in a three-way split, below 50% but enough for the plurality. No runoff mechanism exists in Taiwan's single-round plurality system.

**Scenario B: KMT Surge (Resolves KMT — Tail Risk)**
- **Thesis:** Economic anxiety about housing costs and wages drives undecided voters toward Hou Yu-ih's stability message. Ko's third-party support collapses at the booth as voters engage in strategic voting to defeat DPP.
- **Projected Outcome:** Hou wins 32–35% of the vote in a late surge, narrowly defeating Lai in the popular vote count.

**Scenario C: TPP Overperformance (Resolves TPP — Low Probability)**
- **Thesis:** Ko's youth mobilization dramatically outperforms polling. His digital-native campaign strategy successfully turns out first-time voters at higher rates than traditional surveys predicted.
- **Projected Outcome:** Ko reaches 28–30% of the vote, narrowly edging Hou for second while denying Lai a plurality. However, first-place remains unlikely given DPP's institutional infrastructure advantage.
